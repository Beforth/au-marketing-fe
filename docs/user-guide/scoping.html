Who Sees What
Role Scoping Explained

A short, plain-language guide to how S&M Hub decides which records and dashboard numbers each person can see, and why. No technical knowledge needed.

Prepared byBeForth
Prepared forAureole — Sales & Marketing
DateOctober 2026
Applies toS&M Hub v1.4.9

The idea in one minute

Everyone in S&M Hub signs in with their company ID. The system then answers two separate questions about every person:

1. What can I do?

Create, edit or delete a lead, order or event. This is decided by your permissions, set in HRMS by your administrator. If you lack one, the button simply isn’t there.

2. What can I see?

Which leads, orders, contacts and dashboard numbers appear in your lists. This is decided by your role and your place in the reporting line. This guide is about this question.

The one rule to rememberA record belongs to the person who created it, and it is visible upward to everyone above them in the reporting line. It is never visible sideways to colleagues at the same level, and never downward.

The roles, in simple terms

RoleIn simple terms
Super AdminSees everything across the company. Sets up domains, regions and targets.
Domain HeadResponsible for a whole market (for example Domestic or Export) and sees all of it.
Domain CoordinatorSupports a Domain Head. Sees the whole domain, but cannot change its structure.
Region HeadResponsible for one or more regions and the team working in them.
Region Coordinator / SupervisorMid-level. Sees their own work and their employees’ work, with limited actions.
EmployeeA salesperson. Works on their own records.
Good to knowSomeone who holds the marketing.admin permission (a CEO, for example) can see everything like a Super Admin. That is visibility only. They still need the matching permission for each action, such as deleting a lead.

The reporting line

Leads, Orders, Contacts and Customers all follow the same reporting line. Each person sees what they created, plus what everyone below them created.

ExampleAsha (an Employee) creates a lead. Her Region Head and Domain Head can see it. Her colleague Ravi, also an Employee, cannot, even if they work in the same region.

Three things that adjust the rule

SituationWhat happens
The lead is assigned to youYou can always see it, whoever created it.
A coordinator files a lead on someone’s behalfThat lead is not shared up the normal line. Only the coordinator who filed it, the person it was filed for, the Head of that domain and (if different) the assignee can see it. Super Admin also sees it.
An order is createdThe “creator” is whoever turned the lead into an order. The person who owns the lead keeps sight of the order as the assignee.

Who can choose “Assigned to”: Super Admin (anyone), Domain Head and Coordinator (people in their domain), Region Head (people in their region). Region Coordinators, Supervisors and Employees do not get this field.

An administrator can widen or narrow some of these views with the Visibility settings, for example to let team members see each other’s targets.

Who can change these rules?Permissions and roles are managed by your administrator in HRMS. The reporting-line rules themselves are built into the system, so changing them is a development change, not a switch an administrator can flip.

The dashboard

Every number on a dashboard is filtered to your own scope by the server before it reaches your screen. The same rule applies: you only ever see figures built from records you are allowed to see.

Dashboard itemWhose numbers it shows
Monthly Target and the monthly trendEmployee: your own target. Region Head: your team. Domain Head: your domain. Super Admin: the whole company. The card says which one (“My”, “Region”, “Domain”, “All”).
Follow-ups NeededThe same scope as above. It lists leads that are overdue, due today or due in the next 7 days, from dates people set on a lead and from the automatic “follow up every N days” rule of the lead’s stage.
Leads by Region, Lead Breakdown and lead listsOnly leads inside your scope.
Star Performer of the MonthThe same for everyone: one company-wide winner by value won this month. Domain Heads are not eligible, and the amount is never shown. A lead filed on someone’s behalf counts for the person it was filed for.
Leads around the world (map)Domain Heads and Super Admins only.
Audit logsAdministrators only.
Why one winner for everyone?Star Performer is a recognition card, not a ranking table. Showing only one name, with no amounts, celebrates good work without exposing anyone’s individual figures.

“Why can’t I see this?”

  1. Who created it? If a colleague at your level or below created it, you can’t see it unless you are above them in the line or it is assigned to you.
  2. Is it Won or Lost? Turn on the Won & Lost toggle on the Leads board.
  3. Is a filter on? Check “Only mine”, the date range and the search box.
  4. Is a stage column hidden? “Hide Empty” hides stages with nothing in them.
  5. Missing a button, not a record? That is a permission. Ask your administrator to check your HRMS role.
  6. Past-quarter numbers missing on Domains? That is a Visibility setting. Ask an administrator.
