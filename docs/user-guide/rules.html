2Roles, Permissions & Visibility

2.1Three separate layers

These are often mixed up. They are different systems that answer different questions.

LayerQuestion it answersExampleWhere it is set
PermissionsMay this person perform an action?Create a lead, edit an order, delete an eventHRMS roles and permissions (e.g. marketing.create_lead)
Role scopingWhich rows does a list show?An Employee sees only their own leads; a Region Head sees the team’sBuilt into the server, follows the reporting chain
Visibility SettingsShould past-quarter numbers be displayed?Showing last quarter’s figures on the Domains pageSettings → Visibility (admin allow-list per quarter)
Why they are kept apartVisibility Settings only hide or show history on screen. It is never used to allow or block someone from creating or editing something. Mixing the two once caused a wrong block on back-dated Won dates, which had to be reversed.

2.2How a “yes” is decided

  1. You log in at HRMS and receive a sign-in token plus your permissions and role.
  2. Each page and button checks your permission. If you lack it, the button is hidden or the page shows “Access Denied”.
  3. When you actually perform an action, the server asks HRMS again to confirm the permission. Results are remembered for a short time (a minute or a few), so a freshly granted permission may take a moment to apply.
  4. For lists, the server then filters the rows to your scope (Part 3).
Seeing everything is not the same as doing everythingAnyone holding marketing.admin (for example a CEO) is shown all data like a Super Admin. This is visibility only. They still need the individual permission for each action, such as creating or deleting a lead.

2.3The roles

RoleIn simple terms
Super AdminSees everything and can structure domains, regions and targets.
Domain HeadResponsible for a whole market (for example Domestic or Export). Sees the whole domain.
Domain CoordinatorHelps a Domain Head. Domain-wide view, but view-only on structure.
Region HeadResponsible for one or more regions and the team in them.
Region Coordinator / SupervisorMid-level: sees their own and employees’ work, limited actions. Treated as one tier.
EmployeeA salesperson. Works on their own records.

3Who Sees What

3.1The reporting chain

Leads, Orders, Contacts and Customers all use the same idea: a record belongs to the person who created it, and it is visible upward to everyone above them — never sideways to peers and never downward. The domain or region written on the record no longer decides who sees it.

Records travel up this chain from whoever created them
Record created byWho can see it
Domain HeadOnly themselves
Domain CoordinatorThemselves + Domain Head
Region HeadThemselves + Domain Coordinator + Domain Head
Region Coordinator / SupervisorThemselves + Region Head + Domain Coordinator + Domain Head
EmployeeThemselves + Region Coordinator/Supervisor + Region Head + Domain Coordinator + Domain Head
ExampleAsha (Employee) creates a lead. Her Region Head and Domain Head see it. Her colleague Ravi, also an Employee, does not — even if they are in the same region. Super Admin sees it, and so does whoever the lead is assigned to.

Why: this was a deliberate change from an older model, where every lead and order was visible to everyone in its domain or region. Visibility now follows who created the record and the reporting line above them.

3.2Rules that apply on top

  • The assignee can always see it. If a lead is assigned to you, you see it regardless of the chain.
  • Orders: the “creator” is whoever converted the lead into an order, not necessarily the lead’s creator. If a manager converts an employee’s lead, the order follows the manager’s chain; the employee keeps sight of it only if they are the assignee.
  • Customers: the Account Manager plays the role of the assignee and can always see that customer.
  • Creating an order requires that you can see the underlying lead.
  • Contact → Customer: promoting needs the create-customer permission; a promoted contact can no longer be deleted. The new customer gets its own creator, it does not inherit the contact’s.

3.3Created on someone’s behalf (narrower)

A Domain or Region Coordinator can create a lead for someone in their domain. That lead is deliberately not shared up the normal chain. It is visible only to: (1) the coordinator who filed it, (2) the person it was filed for, and (3) the Head of the lead’s own domain (plus the assignee and Super Admin).

Effect: a coordinator’s filing skips the Region Head and Domain Coordinator and goes straight to the Domain Head.

The server enforces this too: assigning outside your territory is rejected.

3.5Database: Organizations, Customers, Contacts

  • Customers and Contacts follow the reporting chain above.
  • Organizations and Plants still follow territory: you see an organization if it is linked to a contact or customer in your domain or region. So you can sometimes see an organization without being able to open the specific contact that links it.

3.6Events & exhibitions

RoleSees events
Super AdminAll
Domain Head / CoordinatorEvents in their domain(s)
Region Head / Supervisor / CoordinatorEvents in the domain(s) their regions belong to
EmployeeOnly events they are on (selected, travel or hotel list) or created

The booth card-scanning picker is wider on purpose: it lists the caller’s domain events for everyone, so staff working a booth can use it without being formally added. Allocating a visitor to an employee gives that employee the contact; removing the allocation takes it back.

3.7Dashboard

Two layers apply to each widget: whether the role may see the widget at all, and the numbers inside it (always filtered to your scope). Head Summary, Leads by Region, Quotations Submitted and the world map are for Domain Heads and Super Admins only; Audit Logs is for admins. The “Performer of the Month” leaderboard is limited to your own team so there is no company-wide ranking leak.

3.8Service module

Service (contracts, visits, work orders, store, complaints, reports) is controlled by permissions only, not by who created a record. It is a shared, cross-team area, so view access is broad and the actions are split by permission.

4What Happens Automatically

These rules run without anyone clicking anything. Each one lists what happens and why.

4.1Leads and orders

RuleWhat the system doesWhy
Marking a lead WonAsks for the closed value and PO, and optionally a back-dated Won date. Works the same from the board’s Won button, a drag into Won, or Edit Lead → Mark as Won.Sales is often entered a few days late. A back-dated Won date puts the win in the right month.
Won date is the single definition of a winDashboard, MIS and Star Performer all count a win by its Won date.So the same sale never appears in different months in different reports.
Order value changes the leadThe lead’s closed value follows the order’s value, up or down, by the same amount (never below zero, and never below the total of its orders).So targets and reports follow the real order value.
Changing an order’s value needs a reasonA reason of at least 100 characters is required; it is saved in the audit log and the order’s history.Value changes affect targets, so they must be explainable.
Every order status move is loggedOne log entry per move: from-status, to-status, who, when. Title and description are optional; moving to Lost needs a description of at least 100 characters.A clear history, with no gaps and no duplicate entries.
Quotation countingA quotation counts once, at its latest revised price, in the period it was first sent.Revisions should not double count.

4.2Targets, MIS & star performer

RuleWhat the system doesWhy
Targets roll upEmployee targets add up to the region, then to the domain. A region or domain can also carry its own yearly goal; entering 0 clears it.One set of numbers at every level.
Quarter markersThe Domains page shows each quarter as Missed or Active against its target for the financial year.Quick view of where the year stands.
MIS “leads created”A lead counts in the month of its earliest real enquiry or quotation date when that is earlier than the day it was saved. Past months recalculate on their own.A late entry should still count in the month the enquiry really happened.
Star Performer of the MonthOne company-wide winner: the highest total closed value of leads won this month by Won date. Domain Heads are not eligible. The card shows name, region and photo, never the amount.Recognition without exposing individual figures.
Target ring capIf achieved is far above a tiny target, the ring caps at “999%+” and a note says the target looks too low.Avoids unreadable numbers from a mistyped target.

4.3Numbering

Numbering Series builds reference numbers for contacts, customers, leads, enquiries, work orders and job numbers. The pattern builder offers a Financial Year placeholder, {FY}, which becomes the April–March year (for example 2026-27) at the moment the number is generated. “Reset counter” can restart numbering every financial year.

ExampleA pattern of WO/{FY}/{0:4} gives WO/2026-27/0001 for the first work order after 1 April, and restarts at 0001 the next April when the financial-year reset is chosen.

4.4Reminders and notifications

A background job runs about every 30 minutes and sends in-app and push notifications. Each step is sent once, and re-arms if a date moves.

WhatWhen

4.5Settings reload by themselves

When an admin changes marketing settings, the server tells every open screen. Pages refresh their settings-dependent parts without anyone needing to log out.

6Quick Reference & Checklist

6.1“Why can’t I see this?”

  1. Who created it? If a colleague at your level or below you created it, you will not see it unless you are above them in the chain or are the assignee.
  2. Is it Won or Lost? Turn on the Won & Lost toggle on the Leads board.
  3. Is a filter on? Check Only mine, the date range and the search box.
  4. Is a column hidden? Hide Empty hides stages with nothing in them.
  5. Is it a past quarter on Domains? That is a Visibility Setting; ask an admin.
  6. Missing a button, not a record? That is a permission; ask your admin to check your HRMS role.

6.2Things worth remembering

  • A win counts in the month of its Won date, everywhere.
  • A lead’s value follows its order’s value, and changing it needs a 100-character reason.
  • Moving an order to Lost needs a 100-character description.
